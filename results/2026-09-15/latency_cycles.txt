Memory hierarchy latency in cycles, measured directly
M3 Pro, macOS 15.7.9, 2026-09-15

Method: random-permutation pointer chase, 10,000,000 dependent hops,
cycles read from the fixed PMU counter (kperf/kpc). No wall clock and
no frequency conversion anywhere in the calculation. Compiler barrier
(asm volatile "" : "+r"(p)) on every hop. Three untimed warmup passes
inside the binary to clear the DVFS ramp.

buffer      elements    cycles/hop   insns/hop
32 KB           4096         4.005       3.002
256 KB         32768        16.009       3.015
512 KB         65536        21.077       3.020
1 MB          131072        23.180       3.007
2 MB          262144        24.020       3.012
64 MB        8388608       338.519       3.111

Repeatability at 32 KB, three consecutive runs:
  4.005 / 4.011 / 4.018   (spread 0.3%)
  checksum identical (1771) across all runs

insns/hop sits at 3.00 for all in-cache sizes, confirming the loop
compiled to a dependent load chain. The drift to 3.111 at 64 MB is
page-walk machinery appearing in the instruction stream once the
working set exceeds the 2.5 MB TLB reach measured on Day 5.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 1: L1 hit latency is 4.0 cycles, matching Apple's published
figure. The previously OPEN 6.2-cycle anomaly is an artifact.

The 6.2 figure came from dividing a Day 2 nanosecond measurement
(1.54 ns/hop) by a frequency measured on Day 8 (4.021 GHz). The two
came from different runs. 4.021 / (6.19/4.005) = 2.60 GHz, which
matches the cold-core frequency observed in run 1 of today's counter
validation (2.608 GHz).

The Day 2 chase almost certainly ran on a core still ramping under
DVFS. The nanoseconds were recorded correctly; the conversion used
the wrong clock.

Methodology consequence: any cycle count derived from nanoseconds
silently inherits the frequency governor's state at measurement time,
and the error is undetectable without a cycle counter.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 2: the "L2 plateau climb" is L1 residency decay, not an L2
property. Candidate resolution for the PARTIAL entry in surprises.md.

The climb from 16.0 to 24.0 cycles across 256 KB to 2 MB decelerates,
each doubling adding roughly half what the previous one added:
  256 KB -> 512 KB   +5.07
  512 KB -> 1 MB     +2.10
  1 MB   -> 2 MB     +0.84

Every size in this range fits inside the 2.5 MB TLB reach measured on
Day 5 (2 MB = 128 pages against 160 L1 dTLB entries), so TLB misses
cannot be the cause.

Two-population model: with L1 = 4.005 cycles, pure L2 = L, and
fraction f of accesses resident in the 128 KB L1,
  average = f*4.005 + (1-f)*L

  size     f (uniform)   implied L
  512 KB      0.250          26.8
  1 MB        0.125          25.9
  2 MB        0.0625         25.4

Three independent sizes agree within 5%.

ESTIMATE: L2 latency on M3 Pro is approximately 26 cycles.

The 256 KB point (f = 0.5, implied L = 28.0) drifts from the others.
At only 2x L1 capacity the uniform-spread assumption is weakest, since
associativity effects dominate at low capacity multiples (cf. the Day 7
conflict-miss finding).

STATUS: hypothesis with quantitative support, not proven. Confirmation
requires L1D_CACHE_MISS_LD_NONSPEC (event 191) to verify the measured
L1 hit fraction matches the f values above.

FINDING 3: DRAM latency is 338.5 cycles = 84.2 ns at 4.021 GHz.

surprises.md carries an OPEN entry citing 158 ns against an expected
90-100 ns. 84.2 ns falls inside the expected range, suggesting the
same cold-clock artifact as Finding 1.

NOT closed here: the gap is larger than the L1 case and the original
probe should be rerun under counters before the entry is resolved.
